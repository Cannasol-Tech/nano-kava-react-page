## What is nano kava?
<a id="what-is-nano-kava"></a>

Nano kava is a nanoemulsified form of kava extract in which the particles are reduced
to approximately 20 nanometers. Cannasol achieves this with its proprietary
NanoOptimizer™ surfactant system combined with ultrasonic processing on QSonica
equipment. The result is 4–5x higher absorption versus conventional kava powder, so a
formulator can use roughly a quarter of a conventional dose.

The terms **nano kava**, **nano-kava**, **nano emulsified kava**, **nanoemulsified
kava** and **kava nanoemulsion** all describe this same product.

## Nanoemulsification
<a id="nanoemulsification"></a>

Nanoemulsification is the process of breaking oil-based kavalactones into nanometer-scale
droplets suspended in water, so small the mixture becomes almost transparent. Cannasol
runs this process on QSonica ultrasonic liquid-processing equipment — QSonica is the
number-one name in ultrasonic liquid processing. The enormous increase in surface area
is what makes the kavalactones far more absorbable by the body.

## Kavalactone
<a id="kavalactone"></a>

Kavalactones are the characteristic active constituents of the kava plant (*Piper
methysticum*). In traditional preparations they are hydrophobic and poorly absorbed.
Nanoemulsification is what makes them behave like a water-dispersible ingredient.

## NanoOptimizer™
<a id="nanooptimizer"></a>

NanoOptimizer™ is Cannasol's proprietary blend of food-grade surfactants that
encapsulates the kava droplets, keeps them water-dispersible, and prevents them from
re-aggregating. It is the reason the emulsion stays clear and stays suspended, with no
settling and no ring around the bottle.

- Limited absorption in the gastrointestinal tract
- Conventional emulsions run 200–1,000 nm, so they settle and haze the beverage
- Gritty texture and muddy appearance
- Inconsistent dosing leads to unpredictable effects

## Labeling guidance for brand owners

This is guidance for a brand owner's own label, not advice about consuming kava — have
your own label reviewed before print.

Brands dosing in the 50–60 mg range often lead with the weight of the whole nano kava
complex rather than the kavalactone figure alone — a 60 mg dose becomes **"360 mg Nano
Kava Complex"** on the front of the can. The actual kavalactone content is then
disclosed in the Supplement Facts panel, where a consumer or a buyer's regulatory team
can find it.

A panel following 21 CFR 101.36 carries no zero-amount rows, names the plant part,
discloses kavalactone content, and carries the CRN caution wording (abridged). Every
ingredient must appear in Other Ingredients.
